\begin{column}{0.232\paperwidth}

\begin{block}{Motivation}
The HPRC v2.0 minigraph--Cactus graph holds \textbf{233 samples / 464
haplotypes} --- but there has been no interactive way to query it.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A researcher with a \textbf{sequence} cannot ask which haplotypes
        carry it, or where it lies on each of them.
  \item A researcher with a \textbf{position} cannot convert it to another
        assembly: chain files cover only \textbf{56} of the
        $\sim$\textbf{213{,}000} possible sample-to-sample pairs.
\end{itemize}
We built a service that answers both through the graph itself, integrated
into the UCSC Genome Browser.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Three capabilities}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pangenome sequence search} --- a BLAT-like page: a pasted
        sequence is mapped to the whole graph with \texttt{vg giraffe}.
        Results show every assembly carrying the exact allele path, all 464
        haplotypes ranked by identity, and one-click positions on any
        haplotype, drawn as a browser track with base-level mismatch
        coloring.
  \item \textbf{Any-to-any coordinate translation} --- liftover between any
        two of the 466 assemblies at interactive speed, with a scored
        ``which assemblies contain this region'' destination picker.
  \item \textbf{Annotation lifting} --- a QuickLift chain generated
        \emph{at request time}, so users land on an HPRC assembly with
        CHM13's genes, RefSeq, CenSat and T2T Encode at translated
        positions, plus an \textbf{Alignment Differences} track marking
        indels and mismatches between the two assemblies.
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}

\begin{column}{0.232\paperwidth}

\begin{block}{Translation through the graph}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Table 1}: \texttt{contig:start-end} $\rightarrow$ graph
        path + path-local interval.
  \item \textbf{Candidate discovery}: probe nodes on the source path
        ($\sim$1 / 2\,kb); each GBWT probe returns \emph{every} haplotype on
        that node. The \textbf{routing Table 2} replaces probing with a
        direct lookup.
  \item \textbf{Anchoring}: nodes visited exactly once by both sides are
        guaranteed orthologous; a colinearity gate rejects implausible
        pairings.
  \item \textbf{Trace}: an LF-walk between first and last shared node emits
        per-base correspondences.
\end{enumerate}
Results fold into \textbf{chain-semantics blocks}: a block breaks only on
indels, orientation flips, or contig changes --- never on substitutions.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Routing table: 17.2 TB $\rightarrow$ 1.78 GB}
An all-pairs table over the frequency-filtered graph (224M path fragments,
$2{,}367\times$ fragmentation) would cost \textbf{17.2\,TB}. Building over
the non-filtered graph (94{,}554 paths) and storing \emph{routing only} ---
which targets a source range touches, never their coordinates ---
eliminates every failure mode of interval pairing:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & \textbf{interval-pairing} & \textbf{routing} \\
\midrule
abandoned runs & 5{,}025{,}344 & \textbf{0} \\
splits & 35{,}734{,}985 & \textbf{0} \\
segments & 68.2\,M & 45.7\,M \\
size & 3.69\,GB & \textbf{1.78\,GB} \\
build (32 thr) & --- & \textbf{35 min} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Coverage and identity, all 464 at once}
One pass over a mapped read's nodes scores \textbf{every haplotype}
simultaneously (each probe already returns all of them):
\begin{center}
$\displaystyle \mathrm{coverage}(H) = \frac{\textrm{aligned bases on nodes $H$ visits}}{\textrm{aligned bases}}$\\[1ex]
$\displaystyle \mathrm{identity}(H) = \frac{\textrm{\ldots that also \emph{matched} there}}{\textrm{aligned bases}}$
\end{center}
$\mathrm{identity} \le \mathrm{coverage}$ always; the gap is the mismatch
burden. Shown together, they separate ``present but diverged'' from
``half missing'' --- which one score conflates.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{On-the-fly QuickLift chains}
Blocks become bigChain+bigLink on the fly (JSON $\rightarrow$ BED
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{bedToBigBed}), served through a generated hub ---
QuickLift's renderer needs nothing else. \textbf{Two-phase padding} makes
panning work:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Phase 1} (synchronous, 0.7\,s): pad to $5\times$ span,
        clamped to 250\,kb--1\,Mb.
  \item \textbf{Phase 2} (background, 11\,s nobody waits for): a 222-byte
        \texttt{sendBeacon} during navigation rebuilds at $\pm$5\,Mb; the
        next pan picks it up, no reload.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{block}{Performance}
\textbf{Translation latency} (warm, CHM13 chr10 source):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{span} & \textbf{median} & \textbf{completeness} \\
\midrule
100\,bp & 22\,ms & 99.0\% \\
1\,kb & 21\,ms & 99.8\% \\
10\,kb & 115\,ms & 99.6\% \\
100\,kb & 599\,ms & 97.2\% \\
1\,Mb & 4.3\,s & 93.1\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Fixing candidate discovery (stop-at-first-hit $\rightarrow$ exhaustive)
took 1\,Mb completeness from \textcolor{accentred}{\textbf{2.9\%}} to
\textcolor{accentgreen}{\textbf{93.1\%}}, and made it $2\times$ faster.
\vskip1ex
\textbf{Concurrency} (3.4\,kb queries, GIL released in C++):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{threads} & \textbf{q/s} & \textbf{speedup} \\
\midrule
1 & 6.3 & 1.0$\times$ \\
4 & 23.6 & 3.7$\times$ \\
8 & \textbf{60.3} & \textbf{9.6}$\times$ \\
32 & 65.3 & 10.4$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Scored destination picker: 100\,kb region in \textbf{1.28\,s} vs 29.5\,s
exact ($23\times$). Chain generation: linear, $\sim$1.14\,s/Mb.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Future work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bulk offline chain generator}: direct path intersection
        instead of r-index probing --- est.\ $\sim$300$\times$ faster
        ($\sim$1.6\,h per target genome-wide vs 19 days via the API) ---
        for precomputed whole-chromosome chains and full QuickLift parity.
  \item \textbf{Persistent chain cache}, so widened chains outlive the
        one-hour session and popular regions become free after the first
        visit.
  \item Segdup-dense wide intervals lose positions ($\sim$46\% at 1q21.1);
        the bulk path removes the probe spacing that misses them.
  \item Public release: core-change sign-off, per-install API tokens,
        service-side concurrency deployment.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
